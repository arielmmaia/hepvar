# HepVar — Hackathon Submission (100–200 word summary)

HepVar is a clinician-facing rare-variant interpretation tool for hepatology, powered by Claude Opus 4.7. The clinical context: a Brazilian patient receives an exome with a missense in ABCB4 and the sequencing company calls it "VUS — no link to disease established," because ClinVar, OMIM and most variant literature were curated from European cohorts and the relevant case report was published only in Portuguese. HepVar takes the raw genomic coordinates, queries Ensembl VEP, gnomAD v4, ClinVar, UniProt, InterPro, LOVD, AlphaFold-EBI, AlphaGenome, AlphaMissense and PubMed + Europe PMC across five languages in parallel, applies the full ACMG/AMP Tavtigian-Bayesian classifier, then hands the structured evidence object to Opus 4.7 with extended thinking and prompt caching. The model resolves conflicts across sources at differing confidence levels, reads non-English case reports in their original language, and emits an explicit *evidence-gap meta-cognition* section naming the functional assay or family study that would resolve a remaining VUS. A Next.js dashboard streams pipeline progress and reasoning live; conversational follow-up reuses the cached evidence packet at ~95% prefix reuse. Open source, MIT.
